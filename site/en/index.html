About me

I’m Halyna Shpynta. Latin was one of the core languages of my university degree, and from 2007 to 2024 I taught it at Bogomolets National Medical University in Kyiv: to students of medicine, dentistry and pharmacy, to international students and to medical-lyceum pupils.

I wrote a dissertation and a monograph on Latin terminology and co-authored workbooks on Latin and medical terminology, including the Medical Latin Terminology Workbook in English. I also worked in the dean’s office for international students. In 2024 I moved to Czechia with my family, and now I teach online.

My job isn’t just to explain Latin. I bring order to what you’ve already covered, break difficult topics down step by step and show you the logic of medical terms.

How lessons work

  • About 80% of each lesson is practice

    There is theory, of course. But my aim is for you to find the pattern yourself, draw the conclusion and apply it to a new task.

  • Exercises made for you

    I design and choose interactive exercises for each student: some need more examples, some a diagram, some repetition across different tasks.

  • Lessons on Zoom

    Every session is recorded and available for the whole academic year. After a lesson I send PDFs and screenshots when needed.

  • Telegram support 24/6

    Message me with any question between lessons.

  • Finding the gap together

    I don’t just hand you the answer: together we find exactly where the gap is and what stops you understanding it.

  • Honest about vocabulary

    You’ll still have to learn the words. There’s no magic. But you’ll know what you’re learning and why.

Yulia

Dentistry. A case I also want to show you

At the start
Came about a month before the final test, with 2 regular classes and 2 pre-final assessments (written and oral) left.
Lessons
Studied hard and genuinely caught up a lot.
But
Over the semester she had already built up grades of 3 out of 5, unsure answers and little participation in class. Even a sharp improvement just before the test doesn’t always change a result formed over the whole course.

My advice is simple. You don’t have to love Latin. You don’t have to know it perfectly. But show that understanding it matters to you: learn a little at a time, ask questions, answer in class.

That’s why I don’t work on a “let’s learn it all two days before the test” basis. My job is to give you a system you can use to understand and analyse terms on your own.
