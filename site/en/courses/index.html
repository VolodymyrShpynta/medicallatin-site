Main course “Medical Latin” in 2 months

For students of medical, dental and pharmacy faculties in any year, future medical students and medical-lyceum pupils. You won’t just memorise the material: you’ll understand how the terminological language of medicine works.

Next group starts: 11 January 2027

Three levels

Each level includes the one before it: Basis covers grammar, Medium adds prescriptions, and Maxi adds clinical terminology.

LessonBasisMediumMaxi

Basis (Grammatica): lessons 1–⁠8

  1. Bonus: pronunciation of letters and letter combinations. Stress. Capital letters in medical Latin.
  2. Grammar through anatomical terminology. Nomen substantivum: declensions, stem, dictionary form. Non-agreed attributes. Declinatio 1.
  3. Declinatio 1 and Declinatio 2 (masculinum et neutrum).
  4. Nomen adjectivum, classis 1 et 2: stem, declension. Agreed attributes.
  5. Agreed attributes with 1st and 2nd declension adjectives.
  6. Declinatio 3: noun classes and stems. Agreeing group 1 adjectives with 3rd declension nouns.
  7. Declinatio 4–⁠5. Agreeing group 1–⁠2 adjectives with 3rd declension nouns.
  8. Degrees of comparison of adjectives. Declension. Adjectival forms of Latin words.

Medium (Grammatica + Receptum): adds lessons 9–⁠12

  1. Prescriptions. Chemical nomenclature: elements and the names of acids, bases, salts and esters.
  2. Frequent word segments. Drug names and main drug groups.
  3. The structure of a prescription. Prescribing solid dosage forms. Abbreviations and abbreviated prescriptions.
  4. Prescribing soft and liquid dosage forms.

Maxi (Grammatica + Receptum + Clinica): adds lessons 13–⁠16

  1. Clinical terminology. Stress. Word formation: the most frequent prefixes, suffixes and roots.
  2. Building clinical terms with 1st and 2nd declension nouns and adjectives.
  3. Building clinical terms with 3rd declension nouns and adjectives.
  4. The 50 most frequent clinical diagnoses. A rehearsal of the final module test.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really learn medical Latin in 6 days?

Yes, if it’s revision and structure rather than learning from scratch. In 6 days you’ll pull everything into one system and close the gaps.

Is it for me if I don’t understand anything right now?

Yes, that’s exactly who it’s for. We go through the basics in plain words, the most important topics and students’ typical mistakes. It doesn’t make things harder: it explains what was unclear.

Will it help me pass my module test or exam?

Yes, that’s the focus: the key topics that really come up, the logic of declensions and terms, and a structure so you don’t mix things up.

How much time a day does it take, and how long are the sessions?

On average 30–⁠60 minutes a day. Zoom sessions last 60–⁠100 minutes.

Who teaches the course, and why trust them?

A Latin teacher with 17 years at Bogomolets National Medical University and an examiner since 2011. So we’ll practise exactly what exams ask and where students most often go wrong.

Will it close my gaps?

Yes, that’s one of its main goals. You’ll find your weak spots, see where you’re unsure and close them systematically. But you’ll need to learn vocabulary on your own.

Will I be able to read and write prescriptions afterwards?

Yes, if you have basic vocabulary. You’ll understand how a prescription is built and stop fearing Latin in your studies.

How is it different from other courses?

It’s the essentials concentrated into 6 days, focused on students’ typical mistakes, and explained through logic rather than cramming. Nothing extra: only what helps you pass and understand.

“Confident Pronunciation” intensive: 3 days

You know the material but don’t sound sure? In 3 days you’ll learn to read terms without freezing, understand the logic of pronunciation instead of memorised rules, and put the stress in the right place.

Programme

  1. Start reading. Pronouncing every letter and letter combination, with practice from the first lesson.
  2. Stop mixing things up. Stress in Latin words.
  3. Sound like a professional. Professional vocabulary and how it is stressed and read: anatomical, pharmaceutical, chemical and clinical.

How it works

  • 90% of the time is practice: you read aloud constantly, hear yourself and others, and get your pronunciation corrected on the spot.
  • Everyone reads, no exceptions.
  • Mini-groups of 5–⁠10, with personal feedback on pronunciation.
  • Interactive exercises as homework.
  • For the 1–⁠2 most active participants: a free lesson on any problem topic, or 2–⁠3 interactive exercises on difficult vocabulary (up to 30 words), your choice.

Who it’s for

Students of medical, dental and pharmacy faculties, interns, pharmacists and anyone who works with Latin and wants to sound confident.

Trial lesson with a level test

The trial lesson is a chance to get to know each other. We work through tasks from every key topic, and I assess your level and how you take in the material. Afterwards we both know whether we’re a good fit. In it we will:

  • find exactly where your gaps are;
  • work out what stops you understanding the subject;
  • decide how you’ll find it easiest to learn;
  • outline what can be fixed, and roughly how soon.

The level test: five blocks

  1. Pronunciation. Stress.
  2. Nouns. Non-agreed attributes.
  3. Adjectives. Agreed attributes.
  4. Prescriptions. Chemistry. Frequent word segments.
  5. Clinical terminology.

A sample task from block 5. Choose the Latin term for “inflammation of the inner layers of the heart”: endocarditis, pancarditis or exocarditis?
